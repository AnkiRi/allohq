# Prompt for Claude — Joon landing-page visual deliverable

You are the senior brand designer and creative director for Joon. Work
read-only first. Do not write production code and do not produce another theme
reskin. The founder wants an approval-ready visual system and wireframe before
implementation.

## Product truth

Joon is an AI-first retention decision layer for Shopify email. It helps a
merchant decide whom to contact, whom to leave silent, when to send, and what
incremental result followed. It supports merchant-approved campaigns,
email-only journeys, forms, holdouts, and decision receipts. It is not another
cheap bulk email sender. Its repeatable claim is:

> The email tool that gets paid to send less.

The unique visual asset is the **decision receipt**:

> who · what Joon knew · sent or silent · what happened · margin/revenue effect

One receipt is a decision. A stack is a ledger. A sequence is learning.

## Inputs to inspect

Inspect the current production homepage and existing experiments in the repo,
especially the current v2 page, decision-ledger, acquisition-lab, and the
untracked v3 concept if present. Also inspect the real product surfaces for
campaign approval, holdout reporting, customer state, forms, journeys, and the
decision ledger. Treat all metrics and customer examples as illustrative unless
the repository explicitly marks them as real.

Benchmark the current public pages of Klaviyo, Omnisend, and Attentive for
composition, section variety, density, crop strategy, art direction, and mobile
behavior. Do not copy their branding, claims, layouts, or assets. Explain which
observations are current and cite the exact pages/screens reviewed.

## Direction

- Preserve the current v2 narrative argument; replace its visual system.
- Use a light, warm-neutral canvas and one saturated Joon accent.
- Use editorial sans-serif typography; use monospace only for receipt data.
- Make real product UI the illustration: tightly cropped, layered, annotated,
  and filled with credible small details such as names, timestamps, decisions,
  arms, states, and outcomes.
- Give every section a different compositional shape. Avoid a repeated
  text-left/card-right template.
- Own the receipt as Joon's proprietary visual device.
- Use motion only where it explains state change: receipt arrival, a customer
  moving from treatment to holdout, a measured field updating.
- Avoid dark dashboards, drifting particles, generic gradients, glass cards,
  faux operator consoles, palette switchers, logo walls, fake testimonials,
  invented results, stock photography, and decorative 3D.

## Required page story

1. Hero: the claim plus the 30%-off refusal moment within five seconds.
2. Decision-receipt stream: recognizable decisions arriving, including silence.
3. Decision ledger: a customer history where one consequential field changes.
4. Blast tool versus Joon: audience volume versus customer-level judgment.
5. Holdout proof: treatment/control assignment and an honest evidence label.
6. Journeys: an email journey where silence is a first-class node.
7. Acquisition/forms: consent-aware capture and protected incentive issuance.
8. Pricing principle: free v1 and future outcome-aligned pricing, never per email.
9. Founder note: the Zymrat origin story, concise and specific.
10. Capacity-gated CTA for the first design partners.

Use the existing Ankita, Rohan, Meera, and Kavya examples where appropriate.
The strongest interactive scenes are: the discount refusal; silent customer
buys at full price; and Rohan's behavior/state changing over time.

## Deliverables

Return all of the following in one response:

1. A concise visual thesis and five non-negotiable design rules.
2. A desktop wireframe for every section, with dimensions and hierarchy.
3. A mobile wireframe showing how layered product surfaces simplify.
4. A component inventory: receipt, ledger row, holdout card, annotation,
   customer state, campaign approval, journey node, form preview, CTA.
5. One detailed artboard specification for each section: copy role, visual
   object, exact data shown, crop, layering, color, type, and motion.
6. Three materially different hero compositions—not palette variants—with a
   recommendation and tradeoffs.
7. A motion storyboard for the three approved interactive scenes, including
   reduced-motion behavior.
8. A token sheet for color, typography, spacing, radius, shadow, and motion.
9. Accessibility checks: contrast, reading order, keyboard behavior, reduced
   motion, and responsive type.
10. A content-integrity ledger identifying every illustrative number, customer,
    quote, and claim so nothing fabricated reaches production.
11. A production handoff plan mapping each visual to existing product
    components or a clearly named new component.
12. A deletion list for current homepage elements that weaken the new system.

Present the result as a reviewable design brief/wireframe, not code. Make the
differences between hero options obvious in grayscale. Stop after the visual
deliverable so the founder can approve the shapes before implementation.
